02 / BRING YOUR OWN DATA

What should I test next?

Start with the right material. This model learned grid textures, so a random photograph will not give a meaningful inspection.

⌗

Capture a new mesh photo

This is how to try an image that was not in the benchmark.

  1. Use a close-up of similar woven wire mesh. Fill the frame with the texture.
  2. Keep scale, viewpoint and focus similar to the examples. Use steady, even lighting.
  3. Use an original, nearly square photo. Input requirements load with the verified model; resizing can hide small defects.
  4. Upload the image, compare the detector map and separate reconstruction views, and inspect the material yourself.

PNG / JPEG / WebP · ≤12 MiB · 32–4096 px per side · ≤16 MP. Transparent areas are composited on white. Uploads never update the model.

03 / WHEN YOU READ THE RESULT

Compare with a fixed limit

Compare the detector score with its frozen limit. Above the limit suggests review. Unsupported inputs produce no acceptance decision; below-limit scores can still miss defects.

A score, not a probability
What exactly goes into the score?

Verified detector metadata supplies the scoring method and threshold. A decision uses strict score > threshold. Relative map colors do not change the score.

Can I use this for any object?

No. This checkpoint learned the MVTec Grid texture domain. Faces, documents, other materials, and images with different scale or lighting are outside the demonstrated use case.

Is the camera a production system?

It is a live browser demonstration that samples one frame at a time. Delay starts after the previous inspection completes. Camera hardware, line speed, defect coverage and end-to-end latency need separate qualification.

What is saved on my device?

Up to 100 result records, including the filename, score, decision, times and model version. Images are not saved in history. Export CSV or download the current JSON record; clear history when finished.

Why can a defective image look “within range”?

A defect may resemble normal texture, fall below the calibrated limit, or lose detail during resizing. The fixed limit trades false alarms against misses. The autoencoder may also reconstruct defects well; its residual is separate from the active decision. Use known benchmark labels to spot failures.
